# Terms note

## What is on the form now

A required checkbox: "I agree to FreshFold's Terms of Service and Privacy Policy." with links to `/terms` and `/privacy`.

**Those pages do not exist yet.** The links go nowhere. I have no published terms or privacy policy, so a customer cannot actually read what they are agreeing to. This is listed as a defect.

## Where the wording will come from

- **The checkbox sentence** is the standard consent line used on most signup forms. It names both documents and makes acceptance an action (ticking a box) rather than a pre-ticked default.
- **The Terms of Service and Privacy Policy** will be adapted from a published template, not written from scratch by me or by an AI. My plan:
  1. Start from a free template from a reputable terms generator for a small service business, and keep a record of exactly which one and the date.
  2. Adapt it to FreshFold: what I collect (name, email, phone, pickup addresses), why (tagging items, contacting the rider, receipts), how long I keep it, who can see it, how to ask for deletion, and the item-loss and damage policy that the business depends on.
  3. Check it against the **Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023**, which covers personal data of people in Nigeria: lawful basis, consent, data subject rights and breach notification.
  4. Have it checked by someone qualified before FreshFold takes real customers or payments. I do not currently know a lawyer who could check it, and I am recording that as an open risk rather than pretending otherwise.

## Why not let an AI write it

An AI can produce text that *looks* like legal terms. But I can't tell whether it is correct for Nigerian law, whether it promises something FreshFold cannot do, or whether it leaves out something required. Nobody would be accountable for it. Terms are a promise to customers about their data and their belongings; a promise I can't check is not one I should make. "The agent wrote my terms and that's fine" is exactly the wrong answer.
